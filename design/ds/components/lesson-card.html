Answering reveals the correct option, the picked option and an explanation — and, if the pick was right, turns the dot green and advances the progress bar.

Return Per Unit Risk Why a return number means nothing until you divide it by the risk that bought it.+

Two accounts can both finish a window up 4% and be nothing alike: one drifted there, the other swung six percent in a week and happened to land on the same figure. Sharpe collapses that difference into a single number — it takes the mean of the daily excess returns, divides by the standard deviation of that same excess series, and annualizes by multiplying by the square root of 252. The question it answers is not "how much did we make" but "how much were we paid for each unit of wobble we agreed to carry".

The "excess" part is a deliberate choice in this system. The scorecard pulls a real risk-free rate — the latest 3-month T-bill print from FRED, falling back to a manually set environment override and then to zero — and grades the strategy only on return above that rate. Measuring against a 0% baseline while idle cash yields 4-5% would quietly inflate the ratio and tilt the GO/NO-GO toward GO, so the report prints the rate it used and which of those three sources it came from right beside the number.

One shot, then frozen. A pick is written to localStorage['trader_learn'] and every option in that lesson gets disabled. There is no retry, no reset control and no visual difference between a disabled and an enabled option other than the hover — .lopt:disabled only sets cursor:default.

Re-render keeps the disclosure open. Answering rebuilds the whole list from scratch, so the click handler collects the ids of every open <details> first and re-opens them afterwards. More than one lesson can be open at once on web.
